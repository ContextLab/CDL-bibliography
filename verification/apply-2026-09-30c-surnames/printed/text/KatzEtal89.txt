Summary: We present correlations of extent of temporal
lobectomy for intractable epilepsy with postoperative
memory changes (20 cases) and abnormalities of visual
field and neurologic examination (45 cases). Postoperative magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) in the coronal
plane was used to quantify anteroposterior extent of resection of various quadrants of the temporal lobe, using a
20-compartment model of that structure. The Wechsler
Memory Scale-Revised (WMS-R)was administered preoperatively and postoperatively. Postoperative decrease
in percentage of retention of verbal material correlated
with extent of medial resection of left temporal lobe,
whereas decrease in percentage of retention of visual ma-

terial correlated with extent of medial resection of right
temporal lobe. These correlations approached but did not
reach statistical significance. Extent of resection correlated significantly with the presence of visual field defect
on perimetry testing but not with seventy, denseness, or
congruity of the defect. There was no correlation between
postoperative dysphasia and extent of resection in any
quadrant. Assessment of extent of resection after temporal lobectomy allows a rational interpretation of postoperative neurologic deficits in light of functional anatomy
of the temporal lobe. Key Words: Epilepsy-Neurosurgery-Temporal Iobectomy-Prognosis-Complications-Visual fields-Memory.

Temporal lobectomy is a widely accepted surgical treatment for patients with intractable epilepsy
of temporal lobe origin (Delgado-Escueta and
Walsh, 1983; Andermann, 1987). Despite improved
seizure control, there is concern regarding memory
changes (Rausch and Crandall, 1982; Novelly et al.,
1984) and other neurologic deficits (Cahan et al.,
1984; Hahn and Sellers, 1984) which may result
from the procedure. More extensive resections are
presumed to be more likely to result in these complications. However, it is not known to what extent
resection of various temporal lobe structures is responsible for their pathogenesis.
Bilateral temporal lobectomy or unilateral lobectomy in the presence of contralateral temporal pathology can result in significant impairment of
learning and retention of new information (Scoville
and Milner, 1957; Milner, 1974). The impact of uni-

lateral temporal lesions on modality-specific memory function has been equally established in several
studies (Kimura, 1963; Milner, 1974; Ivnik et al.,
1987). However, reports regarding the relative role
of medial and lateral structures in memory deficits
conflict (Milner, 1974; Ojemann and Dodrill, 1985;
Zola-Morgan and Squire, 1985).
Studies that have correlated extent of resection
with memory deficits have relied mostly on intraoperative linear measurements and the surgeon's
assessment of resected structures. Similar estimates of extent of resection have been the basis of
previous correlations with visual field defects
(VFDs) (Penfield and Jasper, 1954; Mario and Rasmussen, 1968; Spencer and Spencer, 1985) and
other neurologic complications after temporal
lobectomy (Penfield and Jasper, 1954; Penfield et
al., 1961; Van Buren et al., 1975). Such estimates of
extent of resection have clear limitations (Awad et
al., 1989).
