Introduction

The basal ganglia play a major role in the regulation of human
movement, as is demonstrated dramatically in Parkinson's
disease, a condition in which dopaminergic denervation of the
striatum leads to paucity and slowness of movement.
Although current anatomical schema of basal ganglia function represent a major advance, they do not wholly explain the
ef®cacy of functional neurosurgery in Parkinson's disease
and this has focused attention on the patterning of neuronal
discharge in the basal ganglia (Marsden and Obeso, 1994;
Obeso et al., 1997; Suarez et al., 1997; Vitek et al., 1999).

(MPTP)-treated primates and in patients with Parkinson's
